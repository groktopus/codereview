# Research-to-design memo: reusable pull-request review harness

**Status:** Proposed design grounded in review guidance, human studies, static-analysis evidence, and current AI-review benchmarks. These sources do not validate this harness or establish AI security competence. The boundaries below distinguish normative guidance, measured evidence, and proposed decisions.

## Normative guidance

OWASP describes baseline and diff-based security review as complementary. A diff review should identify affected components, understand intent, assess existing controls and trust boundaries, and look for new attack paths. Baseline review adds system architecture, data flows, business logic, cryptography, error handling, configuration, and dependencies. OWASP recommends risk-based escalation from diff review when significant concerns appear. A PR review can therefore report scoped findings and request broader analysis, but cannot claim application-wide assurance from the diff alone. ([OWASP Secure Code Review Cheat Sheet](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Secure_Code_Review_Cheat_Sheet.html))

Google’s reviewer guide asks reviewers to understand the assigned change, examine design, functionality, tests, concurrency, complexity, conventions, and context beyond the diff hunk. Where review scope is divided, it says to state which portions were reviewed and to involve qualified reviewers for complex security or concurrency issues. Its standard balances forward progress with code health and favors technical evidence over preference. Smallness means a focused conceptual change, not a line-count formula. ([What to look for](https://google.github.io/eng-practices/review/reviewer/looking-for.html); [review standard](https://google.github.io/eng-practices/review/reviewer/standard.html); [navigation](https://google.github.io/eng-practices/review/reviewer/navigate.html); [small CLs](https://google.github.io/eng-practices/review/developer/small-cls.html)) These are practice guides rather than empirical validation of automated review decisions.

## Measured evidence and limits

Braz et al. randomized 150 online participants across four review treatments: no security instruction, explicit security focus, and focus plus either an OWASP-derived checklist or a tailored checklist. They reviewed one self-contained 160-line Java registration change with two seeded weaknesses. Security instructions were associated with about eightfold higher odds of finding a vulnerability; the checklists did not significantly improve detection over security instructions. This supports making security an explicit lens, but the single change, two weaknesses, and human-review setting do not establish broad or AI detection performance. ([Paper and methods](https://arxiv.org/html/2202.04586); [ICSE record](https://doi.org/10.1145/3510003.3511560))

Charoenwet et al. evaluated five C/C++ analyzers on 815 vulnerability-contributing commits from 92 projects, filtered for language, buildability, and vulnerability metadata. A tool warned in a vulnerable function for 52% of commits. Of 400 manually sampled warnings, 7% were relevant both to the warned line and vulnerability, 76% irrelevant, and 17% uncertain or partly relevant. Warning-based prioritization improved some effort metrics, with results varying by tool and ranking method; 22% of commits had no warning from any studied tool. Vulnerable locations were inferred partly from overlap with fixing commits, not reliable line-level ground truth. The study is bounded to its C/C++ dataset, tools, defaults, and sample; alerts are leads, not proof, and no alert is not evidence of safety. ([Study](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.12241); [ISSTA record](https://doi.org/10.1145/3650212.3680313))

Other studies reinforce the need for context while limiting what can be generalized. Microsoft’s qualitative study observed, interviewed, and surveyed developers and classified hundreds of comments in one industrial setting; it found code/change understanding central to review, alongside knowledge transfer and team awareness. ([Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/expectations-outcomes-and-challenges-of-modern-code-review/)) Ram et al. combined literature review, interviews with ten developers, and ratings of 98 changes by 35 developers; reviewability related to description, size/churn, and coherent history. This concerns ease of review, not defect recall or safe approval. ([ESEC/FSE paper](https://anandsaw.github.io/publications/fse2018.pdf)) A study of four OpenStack and Qt projects found that smell significance and whether to fix it depend on conventions and project context; avoid blanket smell rules or treating cosmetic concerns as blockers. ([Study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.07535))

DeepCRCEval analyzed 100 comments from each of two datasets, combined seven industry interviews with human assessment by experienced graduate students, and focused much of its task analysis on Java. It argues that text similarity is a weak proxy for comment value and identifies relevance, accurate problem identification, explanation, actionability, specificity, context, and completeness as useful dimensions. Its scope and proxy evaluators still limit transfer to production PR decisions. ([Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.18291); [FASE 2025 record](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-90900-9_3))

SWR-Bench v2 covers 1,000 PRs from 12 Python projects, balanced between 500 “Change” and 500 “Clean” cases. “Clean” means no known substantive defect after manual verification and SZZ supplementation, not proof that no latent defect exists. The LLM-based issue matching was checked on a human sample; its balanced benchmark distribution is not natural PR prevalence. Because matching only against known ground truth can label novel valid findings as false positives, harness evaluation needs human adjudication and a natural-prevalence shadow sample. Its authors’ sampled false-positive analysis points to context gaps, but is not a universal model claim. ([Methods and limits](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.01494v2))

## Proposed harness design

### Coverage and depth

Start from immutable base/head revisions and inventory every changed file and logical unit. Review all relevant human-written code and configuration, with generated-data exceptions explicit and verifiable. “Complete changed-surface review” means every unit is understood or marked incomplete, with enough surrounding context to understand enclosing behavior, callers, contracts, and controls. Then add light, focused, or deep effort based on impact, reach, trust-boundary changes, security sensitivity, test evidence, and context uncertainty. These depth names and routing rules are proposals; no source validates specific thresholds for Laya, GLiNER, or another target repository. LOC may inform workload estimates but must not alone set risk, depth, or confidence.

Define deterministic minimum risk floors: inventory all changed paths; cover all changed logical units or name gaps; inspect risk-bearing code/configuration and test modifications; report relevant checks and provenance; and escalate high-impact or specialist-sensitive concerns. Deterministic harness code owns mode selection, stages, budgets, context-fetch scheduling, retry, escalation, termination, and GitHub effects. Validated features feed fixed rules; unknown or unavailable inputs route conservatively. Budget exhaustion preserves missing coverage as incomplete and never implies reviewed-safe.

### Shared core and trusted project profile

Keep the core responsible for orchestration, coverage, evidence capture, transitions, and reporting. Per-project profiles hold trusted conventions, module/symbol/caller/dependency/test maps, interfaces and domain contracts, threat boundaries, approved commands, and execution limits. Provider and tool adapters implement retrieval and checks. Record profile version and provenance at base/head. Fetch context incrementally from changed units outward to callers, implementations, tests, configuration, and boundaries implicated by evidence; missing context is uncertainty, not evidence that a control is absent. The profile must come from trusted repository/maintainer configuration, never instructions added in the PR.

### Bounded model work and finding evidence

System One may answer bounded semantic questions about supplied evidence, such as whether a change touches authorization semantics, whether a cited path supports a proposed finding, or which review lens applies. The harness consumes those answers through deterministic transition rules. Do not ask a generative reviewer to choose workflow stages, next actions, arbitrary tools/commands, or escalation. Its output is structured candidate findings and context-gap proposals, constrained to evidence it received.

Each finding should name the changed location, observed behavior, violated contract or source/control/sink path, plausible consequence, and supporting evidence. Separate observation from inference and assumptions. Classify as substantiated, unsupported, or needs evidence/context. Alerts only prioritize investigation; they do not substantiate a vulnerability. Tie severity to documented reach and impact; do not use universal model-confidence or LOC thresholds. Smells and style feedback should be optional unless a concrete consequence or explicit local rule makes them material.

The final review disposition must remain distinct from merge eligibility. The harness reports what it reviewed, gaps, findings, and disposition; repository branch rules, required CI, owner approvals, and other configured obligations determine merge eligibility. Do not imply every CI failure blocks every repository or that an unrelated pending check was reviewed. Maintain a ledger keyed by base/head SHA: paths, applicable lenses, context sources, checks/results, findings/evidence, skipped work/reasons, profile revision, and disposition. Recheck head before presenting a result; if it changed, mark the review stale and rerun affected coverage.

### Untrusted PRs and evaluation

GitHub warns that executing untrusted PR code under `pull_request_target` may expose cache, secrets, or write privileges; GitHub Security Lab describes the “pwn request” pattern. Treat diff text, PR title/body, comments, filenames, and generated artifacts as untrusted data, not authority over policy. Do not interpolate PR fields into shell commands. Keep retrieval and analysis separate from execution. Run permitted head-code checks in isolation without secrets or write access, using least-privilege tokens. ([GitHub workflow security](https://docs.github.com/en/actions/reference/workflows-and-actions/events-that-trigger-workflows); [Security Lab guidance](https://securitylab.github.com/resources/github-actions-preventing-pwn-requests/))

Evaluate historical clean and bug-introducing PRs with representative held-out repositories/time periods. Include misleading alerts, missing-context cases, changed permissions/configuration, and novel valid findings. Measure missed blockers, false approvals, evidence quality, incomplete disposition, stale-head detection, and coverage—not only latency or cost. Pair known-ground-truth benchmarks with human adjudication of novel findings and a shadow sample at natural prevalence. Optimize cheaper routing only after held-out results show it does not raise missed blockers or false approvals. Exact depth policy and any release gate require their own evaluation; human-review findings do not validate AI review.

## Scope and limits

This memo translates public guidance and bounded studies into a proposal. It does not demonstrate that an AI reviewer can satisfy the proposed contracts, nor does it certify any particular repository. Production release gates require representative, independently adjudicated outcomes and explicit acceptable error and abstention bounds.
